# Portal v2 — Chris review, 14 August 2026

Source: `Call with Chris Lawrie (3).docx` (35m 14s) matched frame-by-frame against the screen
recording — all 106 frames reviewed at 20-second spacing. Timestamps are video positions and are
directly usable: frame 0:00 is the Teams title card and the walkthrough starts at 0:18, matching
the transcript's own clock. Where the cursor or a text selection pins a remark to a specific
element, that is noted.

Verdict in Chris's words: *"It's just UI and workflow — you just need to make it contextually
correct."* The concepts and most of the copy survive; the architecture and the ordering don't.

---

## 1. The one structural rule

Everything else follows from this. **Action first, explanation second, and split the two
audiences onto their own pages.**

> "Anything that is informative should be at the top. The subtext underneath explaining, oh, we
> can do this for you can do that — that is actually a footer to the whole thing." *(8:30)*

> "If you are asking them to read through loads of stuff before there's a call to action, you're
> in trouble, because people get bored very quickly. We're in a world of 20-second consumption
> now." *(16:00)*

> "It's very common in the medical world to go to a website and say: are you a lab or a
> clinician? Literally two big fat buttons in the middle." *(18:40)*

Concretely:

| Now | Should be |
|---|---|
| One long page per hub, seeker + provider stacked, quick-links to jump | Hub landing page → two clearly separated destinations (find capacity / offer capacity), each its own page |
| Explanatory copy above the fold, action below | Action above the fold; the "why" rows immediately after; process/certification detail demoted to the footer of the page |
| Print Hub and Mill Hub laid out differently | Identical pattern on both — *"they're both the same thing, just one of them is mill, one of them is print"* |

On the seeker/provider split, twice, in both hubs:

> "You're on a different thread, which is about your printer. I don't want to treat you like
> someone looking — I want to treat you differently." *(9:00, print hub)*

> "The difference between somebody who's looking for mill capacity and those that have mill
> capacity to offer is too subtle. It's hidden down here out of the way." *(17:20, mill hub)*

## 2. Print Hub (`print-hub.html`)

Resolved from two passes over the same question (5:20 and 9:40): Chris first wanted the capacity
finder at the very top, then on a second look decided the "why would I bother" rows earn that
slot because they create the curiosity that makes the finder worth using.

2. **Priority 1** — the four opportunity rows (currently "Built for the labs the printer market left in between")
3. **Priority 2** — Find capacity near you
4. Everything else (how it works, certification, network band) as footer-weight subtext

> "I'm curious about this — why would I do it? I can print across borders. I never thought that.

### 🐞 The finder returns the same three nodes for every region — `portal.js:92–94`
Not something Chris named, but the recording shows it plainly and it undercuts the whole demo.
The results are a single hardcoded three-node array; only the intro sentence above the cards
varies by region. Australia & New Zealand at 6:20 returns HP Multi Jet Fusion / Formlabs Fuse 1 /
Formlabs Fuse X1 at 250, 420 and 600 miles — and North America → Canada at 28:00 returns the
identical three cards at the identical distances.

Chris probed exactly this and was reassured it was placeholder: *"How did you know the distance?
Is it worked out where they are, or is it just a test at the moment?"* — *"It's just a test."*
*(6:40)* That answer holds for one demo, but the map is the centrepiece on Monday, and a map that
returns Canada's nodes for New Zealand will be spotted in seconds. It also compounds the
vendor-naming problem below: every region currently shows HP and Formlabs.

### Carry the location forward into the register form — `print-hub.html:197–198`
> "I don't want to put that in again. You've led me to an action, you've given me information
> without me having to do much. Now I'm willing to go forward — but you asked me to type my

Country and City/Town empty again. The complaint lands because he had just done the work.

Same point applies to the intro modal.

### "Own printers? Found the network on them" — `print-hub.html:171`
Wording is odd but the intent reads (*"do you want to become a host of a hub"*). Move the whole
block to its own page, and **repeat the location step there** — the provider journey gets the
same treatment as the seeker journey, not a form at the bottom of someone else's page.

## 5. Imagery

Direction approved, execution not. All current images are AI-generated placeholders and two are
technically wrong in ways an O&P audience will spot immediately.

- **Mill hero (`mill-hub.jpg`) — wrong process.**
  > "It's understood it's plastic, but it's treating it like it's a soft-material mill. Plastic
  > ones are milled *inside* the material, with pins holding it in. This is showing your machine
  > all the way around it — that would be massively inefficient. **This wall here doesn't get
  > milled down. It's just a cavity that it mills out.**" *(29:20 — cursor on the hero image)*
- **Print imagery — show shells, not finished insoles.** *(29:40–30:20, cursor on the print hero)*
  Chris will photograph real shells, and can supply an image of how the Dean system runs at
  iOrthotics as a reference.
- **Realism.** *"That does not look like a lab, it looks like a flat place. We're better off
  having much more realistic images."* Route agreed: photograph the real parts, feed them to
  Higgsfield as style/content references, regenerate.
- **Education hub image — remove the software from the monitor.**
  > "It's very relevant. The problem is it's got software in it. We need to get software out.
  > I've got Jason problems there — he'll go, who the hell is that, is that another company
  > trying to sell someone else's software? Got to keep the paranoia out of it."
  > *(24:00 — cursor resting directly on the monitor in the image)*

  The pressure-mat alternative was also rejected (it's an insole *design* image).
- **Fit360 in the imagery — open question for you.** Chris talked himself round to it:
  > "We cannot start promoting any of the software, but there's nothing stopping us from building
  > on Fit360 as well. The more trust there is in us for doing this, the more Fit360 opportunity
  > there is… I don't think Jason will have an issue with that. His biggest fear is this will eat
  > Fit360 alive." *(25:40)*
- **Vendor neutrality is deliberate and approved.** The fictional SLS machine is fine precisely
  because it names nobody: *"I don't want MultiJet. We don't know if MJF is going to work with
  us, HP. We don't know if anything's going to happen with Formlabs."*

  ⚠️ **This contradicts the live draft.** The results cards name *HP Multi Jet Fusion*,
  *Formlabs Fuse 1* and *Formlabs Fuse X1*, and the register form placeholder reads
  *"HP MJF 5200 · Nylon 11, TPU"*. Needs a decision before the next version.

per-region counts would have been a lie.
